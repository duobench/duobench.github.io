Abstract

Bimanual robot systems substantially expand manipulation capabilities, but coordinating two arms introduces additional control complexity and failure modes that are not well captured by existing benchmarks. We introduce DuoBench, an extensible benchmarking framework for bimanual manipulation policies on the Franka Research 3 Duo platform. DuoBench comprises eleven tasks spanning four coordination categories, implemented in simulation and partially reproduced in the real world through reproducible task recipes with 3D-printable assets. In addition, we propose a stage-based evaluation scheme that supports fine-grained semantic failure analysis beyond binary success, including stage-conditional pass rates with confidence intervals, and provide human-teleoperated datasets for all benchmark tasks. We benchmark several dual-arm imitation-learning and vision-language-action policies in simulation and on real hardware. Our results show that current policies remain challenged by bimanual manipulation, particularly in early interaction stages, parallel arm execution, and transfer between simulation and real-world settings, and that simulation locates the same bottlenecks as the real robot. DuoBench provides a reproducible testbed for diagnosing these failure modes and studying future methods for dual-arm policy learning.

Motivation & Main Contribution

Why DuoBench?

Existing manipulation benchmarks still provide limited support for systematically evaluating dual-arm coordination. They often underrepresent the diversity of coordination patterns required by two-arm manipulation, provide little support for reproducible sim-and-real evaluation, or rely mainly on binary task success.

That makes it hard to tell whether failures come from grasp acquisition, stabilization, object transfer, parallel execution, or later semantic stages of the task.

What DuoBench adds

  • 11 bimanual benchmark tasks across 4 coordination categories
  • Stage-based evaluation with stage-conditional pass rates and confidence intervals for semantic failure analysis
  • Reproducible sim-and-real task recipes with 3D-printable assets
  • Teleoperated datasets and a shared interface for collection, replay, and evaluation

Platform & Reproducibility

DuoBench is built around the Franka Research 3 Duo setup and exposes the same benchmark logic through a shared environment interface for simulation, teleoperation, replay, and evaluation.

The goal is not just to report whether a policy succeeds on a single lab setup, but to make the benchmark itself reproducible across labs. Tasks can be recreated in simulation and, for a subset, in the real world with 3D-printable assets and shared teleoperation tools.

  • duobench/<task_id> Gymnasium environments
  • Task stages directly exposed through the environment wrapper
  • Human teleoperation in simulation and on hardware
  • Replay support for future ablations and extensions

Bimanual Task Taxonomy

Asymmetric Support

One arm stabilizes or holds an object while the other arm interacts with it indirectly, e.g. holding a lid open while the other arm inserts a cube.

Hinge-Chest, Spring-Door, Pour-Marbles

Bimanual Manipulation

Both arms jointly manipulate the same object, forming a closed kinematic chain, e.g. lifting a heavy pot.

Ball-Maze, Carry-Pot, Block-Balance, Join-Blocks

Sequential Handoff

An object is transferred between both arms due to workspace or task constraints; the role of the acting arm changes at the handover.

Transfer-Cube, Transfer-Gate, Transfer-Reorient

Parallel Execution

Both arms execute independent unimanual tasks, simultaneously or sequentially, without direct interdependence.

Bin-Sort

Simulation Results

We evaluate three representative policies with dual-arm support: ACT as a task-specific imitation-learning baseline, and the vision-language-action models π0.5 and X-VLA. ACT is trained per task on 50 demonstrations, while the VLAs are trained on all 550 simulation episodes with task-instruction conditioning. Each policy is evaluated for 300 rollouts per task.

DuoBench is far from saturated. Across policies, many failures already occur in the earliest stages: initial grasp acquisition and task setup remain the dominant bottlenecks even before more complex coordination is required. The bottleneck is a stage that requires establishing a grasp in 22 of the 33 task-policy combinations.

Parallel execution is especially difficult: Bin-Sort remains a weak point across policies, suggesting that current policies struggle with independent control of both arms and with demonstrations that admit multiple valid execution orders.

Mixed simulation-and-real training can help on some real-world tasks, but the sim-to-real gap remains clearly visible. That makes DuoBench useful not only for comparing policies within a domain, but also for studying transfer strategies and data-mixing choices.

  • Early-stage failures, especially grasp acquisition, dominate many rollouts
  • Transfer-Gate (23–43%) is easier than unconstrained Transfer-Cube (9–13%): constraining the handover makes it more predictable
  • Pour-Marbles, Block-Balance and Transfer-Reorient stay at or near 0% for all policies
  • Bin-Sort (at most 1.7%) exposes persistent weakness in parallel execution
  • No uniform ranking: ACT leads on Carry-Pot and Transfer-Gate, X-VLA on Join-Blocks

Real-World Results

Real-world evaluation on the four replicated tasks shows the same overall pattern as simulation. Many failures happen early, with grasping and initial setup remaining the dominant bottlenecks. At the same time, once grasping succeeds, later stages are often completed reliably, especially in tasks such as Transfer-Cube and Bin-Sort.

Simulation vs. Real World

Stage-conditional pass rates locate the same bottlenecks in both domains. In Ball-Maze, the bimanual grasp is reliable in simulation and on the real robot (266 to 284 of 300 simulated, 10 to 11 of 15 real episodes), and guiding the ball to the goal is the bottleneck, succeeding in 2 to 9% (sim) and 0 to 27% (real) of grasped episodes. In Transfer-Cube, picking up the cube is the bottleneck in simulation for all policies and on the real robot for ACT and X-VLA (0 and 1 of 15), whereas π0.5 picks more reliably on the real robot (11 of 15) than in simulation (140 of 300). Simulation thus locates bottlenecks faithfully even where it misestimates success rates.
